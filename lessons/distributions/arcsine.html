Arcsine

scipy.stats.arcsine

Intuition

The arcsine distribution describes quantities that spend most of their 'time' near the edges of a range and least near the middle -- famously, the fraction of time a random walk (like a coin-flipping fortune) spends on the positive side.

Formula

\[ f(x) = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{x(1-x)}}, \quad 0 < x < 1 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning

Use cases

  • Fraction of time a random walk spends positive (arcsine law)
  • Modeling proportions clustered near 0 or 1, e.g. voltage in an AC cycle

A funny example

Two kids play a long game of tug-of-war where the rope randomly drifts back and forth. If you track what fraction of the whole match one kid was 'winning,' it's surprisingly rare for that fraction to be near 50-50 -- much more often one kid was ahead almost the whole time, or almost none of it.

What's the probability that one kid was ahead for less than 10% or more than 90% of the match?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.arcsine()
print('P(X<0.1 or X>0.9):', round(dist.cdf(0.1) + dist.sf(0.9), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(X<0.1 or X>0.9): 0.4097 mean: 0.5
