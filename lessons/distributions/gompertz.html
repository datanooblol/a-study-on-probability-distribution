Gompertz

scipy.stats.gompertz

Intuition

The Gompertz distribution models a hazard rate that grows exponentially with time -- the classic pattern of human aging, where your risk of dying keeps accelerating the older you get, which is exactly why it's used in actuarial mortality tables.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = c\,e^x\exp\!\left(-c(e^x-1)\right), \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c1shape parameter controlling how quickly the mortality/hazard rate rises

Use cases

  • Actuarial mortality and life-table modeling
  • Modeling accelerating failure/hazard rates in aging populations

A funny example

A kid's grandparent explains that as people get older, the chance something goes wrong with their health each year climbs faster and faster, unlike a young kid's fairly constant, low, day-to-day risk.

What's the probability a person (in these standardized age units) survives past age 1.5, and what's their expected lifetime?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.gompertz(c=1)
print('P(survives past 1.5):', round(dist.sf(1.5), 4))
print('expected lifetime:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(survives past 1.5): 0.0308 expected lifetime: 0.5963
