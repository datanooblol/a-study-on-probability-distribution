Exponentially Modified Normal

scipy.stats.exponnorm

Intuition

This is what you get when you add a normal random variable to an independent exponential one -- a Gaussian bump with one side dragged into a long exponential tail, exactly the shape seen in chromatography peaks and reaction-time data.

Formula

\[ f(x;K) = \frac{1}{2K}\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2K^2}-\frac{x}{K}\right)\text{erfc}\!\left(-\frac{x-1/K}{\sqrt2}\right) \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
K1.5ratio of the exponential's mean to the normal's std dev, stretching the tail

Use cases

  • Human reaction-time modeling in psychology experiments
  • Chromatography peak shape analysis in analytical chemistry

A funny example

A kid's reaction time in a game is normally quick and consistent, but sometimes their mind wanders for an extra random exponential delay before they press the button, dragging out the slow end of their times.

What's the probability their reaction time exceeds 2 seconds, and what's their average reaction time?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.exponnorm(K=1.5)
print('P(time > 2s):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('mean reaction time:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(time > 2s): 0.3219 mean reaction time: 1.5
