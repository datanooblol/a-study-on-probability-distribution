Zipf (Zeta)

scipy.stats.zipf

Intuition

Zipf's distribution is the discrete power law behind Zipf's law: the most common word in a language is used roughly twice as often as the second-most-common, three times as often as the third, and so on -- rank-1 items dominate, and popularity drops off fast but with a long tail.

Formula

\[ f(k;a) = \frac{1/k^a}{\zeta(a)}, \quad k=1,2,3,\ldots \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a2.5shape parameter (tail exponent), a > 1

Use cases

  • Word frequency modeling in linguistics and natural language processing (Zipf's law)
  • City population rank-size distribution modeling

A funny example

A kid counts how often each word appears in their favorite book. The word 'the' shows up constantly, the next most common word shows up about half as often, and so on down a long tail of words that barely appear at all.

What's the probability a randomly picked word instance is the single most common word (rank 1)?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.zipf(a=2.5)
print('P(rank = 1):', round(dist.pmf(1), 4))
print('P(rank <= 5):', round(dist.cdf(5), 4))
P(rank = 1): 0.7454 P(rank <= 5): 0.9617
