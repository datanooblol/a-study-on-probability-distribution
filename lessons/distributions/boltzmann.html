Boltzmann

scipy.stats.boltzmann

Intuition

This is the discrete, truncated version of the exponential distribution -- named for the physics of energy levels in a system at thermal equilibrium, where lower energy states are exponentially more likely than higher ones, but there's only a finite number of levels available.

Formula

\[ f(k;\lambda,N) = \frac{(1-e^{-\lambda})e^{-\lambda k}}{1-e^{-\lambda N}}, \quad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
lambda_1decay rate parameter (higher = faster drop-off toward low values)
N10number of possible outcome values, 0 through N-1

Use cases

  • Statistical mechanics: energy level occupation at thermal equilibrium
  • Modeling truncated exponential-decay count data with a hard maximum

A funny example

A video game has 10 possible power-up levels a mystery box can drop, numbered 0 through 9. Low-numbered (common) power-ups drop far more often than high-numbered (rare) ones, following an exponential-style drop-off, but the box can never drop level 10 or higher because it doesn't exist.

What's the probability the mystery box drops a power-up at level 2 or lower?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.boltzmann(lambda_=1, N=10)
print('P(level <= 2):', round(dist.cdf(2), 4))
print('mean level:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(level <= 2): 0.9503 mean level: 0.5815
