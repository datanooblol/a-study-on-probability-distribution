Rice

scipy.stats.rice

Intuition

The Rice distribution is the magnitude of a 2D vector with a fixed nonzero 'signal' component plus independent normal noise in both directions -- it's exactly what happens to radio or radar signal strength when a real signal is present but corrupted by Gaussian noise (it reduces to Rayleigh when there's no signal at all).

Formula

\[ f(x;b) = x\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2+b^2}{2}\right)I_0(xb), \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
b2noncentrality shape parameter, ratio of signal strength to noise std dev

Use cases

  • Radar and radio signal detection with Gaussian noise (signal-plus-noise magnitude)
  • MRI image magnitude noise modeling in medical imaging

A funny example

A kid uses a metal detector that's genuinely picking up a buried coin (a real signal), but the reading also jitters around from random electrical noise in both the north-south and east-west directions -- the detector's total reading strength follows the Rice distribution.

What's the probability the detector reading exceeds 3?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.rice(b=2)
print('P(reading > 3):', round(dist.sf(3), 4))
print('mean reading:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(reading > 3): 0.2144 mean reading: 2.2724
