R-distribution

scipy.stats.rdist

Intuition

The R-distribution is the exact distribution of a single coordinate of a random point picked uniformly on the surface of a c-dimensional sphere -- it's bounded between -1 and 1 and its shape parameter directly reflects how many dimensions that sphere lives in.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \frac{(1-x^2)^{c/2-1}}{B(1/2,\,c/2)}, \quad -1 \le x \le 1 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c1.5shape parameter

Use cases

  • Directional statistics: coordinate distribution of points on a hypersphere
  • Correlation coefficient sampling distribution derivations

A funny example

A kid spins a bubble-shaped ball randomly and marks where a fixed dot on its equator ends up, projected flat onto a ruler from -1 to 1. Where that dot lands follows the R-distribution, shaped by how many dimensions the 'ball' really has.

What's the probability the mark falls between -0.5 and 0.5?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.rdist(c=1.5)
print('P(-0.5 < X < 0.5):', round(dist.cdf(0.5) - dist.cdf(-0.5), 4))
print('variance:', round(dist.var(), 4))
P(-0.5 < X < 0.5): 0.427 variance: 0.4
