Fatigue Life (Birnbaum-Saunders)

scipy.stats.fatiguelife

Intuition

Derived from a physical model of material fatigue -- microscopic cracks accumulate randomly with each stress cycle until one crack crosses a critical length and the material breaks -- so it's the go-to distribution for time-to-failure under repeated stress.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \frac{x+1}{2c\sqrt{2\pi x^3}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-1)^2}{2xc^2}\right), \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c1.0shape parameter related to the coefficient of variation of cycles-to-failure

Use cases

  • Metal fatigue and structural failure-time analysis in engineering
  • Reliability testing of components under cyclic stress

A funny example

A kid bends a paperclip back and forth over and over, and tiny invisible cracks build up with each bend until, on one unpredictable bend, it finally snaps in two.

What's the probability the paperclip survives more than 2 (scaled) bends, and what's the expected number of bends before it breaks?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.fatiguelife(c=1.0)
print('P(survives > 2 cycles):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('expected cycles to break:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(survives > 2 cycles): 0.2398 expected cycles to break: 1.5
