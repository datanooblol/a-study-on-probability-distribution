Log-Uniform (Reciprocal)

scipy.stats.loguniform

Intuition

A distribution that's uniform on the logarithmic scale rather than the linear scale -- it gives equal probability to the range [1,10] as to [10,100] as to [100,1000], which is exactly the right prior when you know a quantity spans several orders of magnitude but have no idea which one.

Formula

\[ f(x;a,b) = \frac{1}{x\ln(b/a)}, \quad a \le x \le b \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a1lower bound of the support
b10upper bound of the support

Use cases

  • Hyperparameter search priors spanning orders of magnitude in machine learning
  • Bayesian priors for scale parameters with unknown order of magnitude

A funny example

A kid is asked to guess how many grains of sand are in a jar, somewhere between 1 and 10,000. Since they truly have no idea whether it's in the hundreds or the thousands, guessing 'uniformly on the number of digits' (log-uniform) makes more sense than guessing uniformly on the raw count.

What's the probability the guess falls between 1 and 3 (versus the full range up to 10)?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.loguniform(a=1, b=10)
print('P(1 < X < 3):', round(dist.cdf(3) - dist.cdf(1), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(1 < X < 3): 0.4771 mean: 3.9087
