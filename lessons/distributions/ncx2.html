Noncentral Chi-Squared

scipy.stats.ncx2

Intuition

If you sum k squared normal variables that each have a nonzero mean instead of zero mean, you get a noncentral chi-squared -- the noncentrality parameter lambda captures how far those means are from zero, and it's the distribution behind statistical power calculations for chi-squared tests.

Formula

\[ f(x;k,\lambda) = \frac12 e^{-(x+\lambda)/2}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{k/4-1/2}I_{k/2-1}\!\left(\sqrt{\lambda x}\right), \quad x > 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
df5degrees of freedom
nc3noncentrality parameter

Use cases

  • Statistical power analysis for chi-squared goodness-of-fit tests
  • Signal detection theory (radar/sonar signal-plus-noise power)

A funny example

A kid throws 5 darts at a target, but their aim is slightly off-center rather than perfectly centered. The total squared distance of all 5 darts from the bullseye follows this noncentral chi-squared shape instead of the usual centered version.

What's the probability the total squared distance exceeds 10?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.ncx2(df=5, nc=3)
print('P(total > 10):', round(dist.sf(10), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(total > 10): 0.2828 mean: 8.0
