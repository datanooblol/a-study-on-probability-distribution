Trapezoidal

scipy.stats.trapezoid

Intuition

A ramp up, a flat plateau, then a ramp down -- trapezoidal is the natural choice when you're confident a quantity lies somewhere in a central 'most likely' range but want to allow gradually tapering probability toward the edges, a common expert-elicitation shape in risk analysis.

Formula

\[ f(x;c,d) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{2x}{c(d-c+1)}, & 0 \le x < c \\[6pt] \dfrac{2}{d-c+1}, & c \le x < d \\[6pt] \dfrac{2(1-x)}{(1-d)(d-c+1)}, & d \le x \le 1 \end{cases} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c0.3start of the flat plateau, as a fraction of the support
d0.7end of the flat plateau, as a fraction of the support

Use cases

  • Risk analysis and expert-elicited uncertainty modeling (project scheduling)
  • Fuzzy logic membership function construction

A funny example

A kid guesses how long a movie will take to finish loading: it's very unlikely to load instantly or take forever, and there's one central stretch of time (the plateau) where it's equally likely to finish at any moment.

What's the probability the movie finishes loading during the flat 'most likely' plateau, between x=0.3 and x=0.7?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.trapezoid(c=0.3, d=0.7)
print('P(0.3 < X < 0.7):', round(dist.cdf(0.7) - dist.cdf(0.3), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(0.3 < X < 0.7): 0.5714 mean: 0.5
