Gumbel (left-skewed / minimum)

scipy.stats.gumbel_l

Intuition

Gumbel-left is the mirror image of the standard Gumbel: it describes the distribution of the minimum of many independent random values, with a sharp cutoff on the right and a long tail stretching left -- the natural home for 'weakest link' failure modeling.

Formula

\[ f(x) = e^{x}\exp\!\left(-e^{x}\right), \quad x \in \mathbb{R} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning

Use cases

  • Weakest-link material strength and failure modeling
  • Modeling minimum values in extreme-value theory (e.g. lowest annual temperature)

A funny example

A chain is only as strong as its weakest link, and out of a hundred links, the strength of the very weakest one follows this left-skewed shape -- most chains have a fairly predictable weak point, but occasionally a chain has one especially fragile link.

What's the probability the weakest link's strength falls below -1 (in standardized units)?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.gumbel_l()
print('P(X < -1):', round(dist.cdf(-1), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(X < -1): 0.3078 mean: -0.5772
