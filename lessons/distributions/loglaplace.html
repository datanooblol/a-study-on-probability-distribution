Log-Laplace

scipy.stats.loglaplace

Intuition

Just as the Laplace distribution is a sharp double-exponential peak on the linear scale, log-Laplace is that same sharp peak transferred onto a multiplicative (log) scale -- it's a popular alternative to the lognormal for financial returns and city/firm sizes because it has a sharper peak and heavier tails.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{c}{2}x^{c-1}, & 0 < x < 1 \\[4pt] \dfrac{c}{2}x^{-c-1}, & x \ge 1 \end{cases} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c3shape parameter controlling how sharply the peak falls off on the log scale

Use cases

  • Alternative to lognormal for financial asset price and return modeling
  • City and firm size distribution modeling with sharper peaks

A funny example

A kid tracks the price of a rare trading card over months. It usually stays close to a typical price, but the log-Laplace shape means sudden multiplicative jumps up or down happen more dramatically than a smooth lognormal curve would suggest.

What's the probability the card's price ratio exceeds 2 (i.e. more than doubles)?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.loglaplace(c=3)
print('P(price ratio > 2):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('median price ratio:', round(dist.median(), 4))
P(price ratio > 2): 0.0625 median price ratio: 1.0
