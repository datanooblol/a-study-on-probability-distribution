Discrete Laplace

scipy.stats.dlaplace

Intuition

The integer-valued cousin of the Laplace distribution: a sharp peak at zero with two symmetric, exponentially-decaying tails in both directions -- a natural model for rounding errors or integer-valued noise that's more likely to be small than large, in either direction.

Formula

\[ f(k;a) = \tanh(a/2)\,e^{-a|k|}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a0.8decay rate parameter (higher = tighter clustering around zero)

Use cases

  • Discretized noise modeling for digital signal quantization error
  • Integer-valued financial tick-size and rounding error modeling

A funny example

A kid counts how many steps off-target their beanbag toss lands, left or right of a target line drawn on the ground, only in whole-step units. Small misses (in either direction) are common, big misses get rarer and rarer symmetrically.

What's the probability the beanbag lands within 2 steps of the target line, in either direction?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.dlaplace(a=0.8)
print('P(|steps off| <= 2):', round(dist.cdf(2) - dist.cdf(-3), 4))
print('variance:', round(dist.var(), 4))
P(|steps off| <= 2): 0.8748 variance: 2.9635
