Skellam

scipy.stats.skellam

Intuition

Skellam is the distribution of the difference between two independent Poisson-counted quantities -- exactly the shape you get when you subtract 'goals scored by team B' from 'goals scored by team A,' where each team's scoring follows its own Poisson process.

Formula

\[ f(k;\mu_1,\mu_2) = e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)}\left(\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}\right)^{k/2}I_k\!\left(2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2}\right) \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
mu14mean of the first (positive-contributing) Poisson process
mu22mean of the second (negative-contributing) Poisson process

Use cases

  • Sports score-difference modeling (goal difference in soccer/hockey)
  • Modeling count differences between two independent Poisson processes

A funny example

Two soccer teams each score goals at their own steady average rate throughout a season of games. A kid tracks the difference in goals (team A's goals minus team B's goals) for each match, which follows the Skellam distribution.

What's the probability team A wins by exactly 2 goals?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.skellam(mu1=4, mu2=2)
print('P(win margin = 2):', round(dist.pmf(2), 4))
print('expected margin:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(win margin = 2): 0.1658 expected margin: 2.0
