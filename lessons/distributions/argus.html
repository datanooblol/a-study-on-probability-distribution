ARGUS

scipy.stats.argus

Intuition

Built by particle physicists at the ARGUS experiment, this distribution models the reconstructed mass of particle decay background: it starts at zero at both ends of a bounded range and bulges in the middle, with the bulge's sharpness set by chi.

Formula

\[ f(x;\chi) = \frac{\chi^3}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Psi(\chi)}\, x\sqrt{1-x^2}\, \exp\left(-\frac{\chi^2(1-x^2)}{2}\right), \quad 0 < x < 1 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
chi1.0curvature parameter controlling how sharply the density peaks and falls to zero at x=1

Use cases

  • Modeling combinatorial background mass spectra in particle physics
  • Bounded 'peaked in the middle, zero at both ends' phenomena

A funny example

A candy machine drops a gumball that rolls down a curved ramp of fixed length. It almost never stops right at the very start or the very end of the ramp -- it usually rolls to somewhere comfortably in the middle before friction stops it.

What's the probability the gumball stops in the middle third of the ramp (between x=0.33 and x=0.67), and where does it stop on average?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.argus(chi=1.0)
print('P(0.33<X<0.67):', round(dist.cdf(0.67) - dist.cdf(0.33), 4))
print('mean stop point:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(0.33<X<0.67): 0.4023 mean stop point: 0.6187
