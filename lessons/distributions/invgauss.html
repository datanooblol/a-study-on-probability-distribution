Inverse Gaussian (Wald)

scipy.stats.invgauss

Intuition

Despite the name, this has nothing to do with the normal distribution's shape -- it's the exact distribution of the first time a random walk with a steady drift crosses a fixed level, which is why it naturally models 'time to reach a goal' processes.

Formula

\[ f(x;\mu) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi x^3}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\mu^2 x}\right), \quad x > 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
mu1mean of the distribution, in units of the scale

Use cases

  • First-passage time modeling for drift-diffusion processes
  • Modeling reaction times and response latencies in cognitive science

A funny example

A snail crawls toward a lettuce leaf, drifting steadily forward but wobbling randomly side to side and even backward sometimes. The inverse Gaussian distribution is exactly how long it takes the snail to finally reach the leaf.

What's the probability the snail reaches the leaf within 1.5 time units?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.invgauss(mu=1)
print('P(reaches leaf by t=1.5):', round(dist.cdf(1.5), 4))
print('mean time:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(reaches leaf by t=1.5): 0.8108 mean time: 1.0
