Generalized Extreme Value

scipy.stats.genextreme

Intuition

The single distribution that describes the maximum of many independent random values, no matter what those values' original distribution was -- it unifies the Gumbel, Frechet, and Weibull extreme-value families under one shape parameter c, which is why it's the backbone of flood, heatwave, and market-crash risk modeling.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \exp\!\left(-(1-cx)^{1/c}\right)(1-cx)^{1/c-1}, \quad c \ne 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c0.1shape parameter (0 gives Gumbel-like, positive gives bounded, negative gives heavy tail)

Use cases

  • Flood, heatwave, and extreme weather risk analysis in climate science
  • Financial risk modeling of maximum losses (extreme value theory)

A funny example

Every year a town records its single hottest day. Whatever the day-to-day weather looks like, the yearly record-high temperatures themselves follow this one universal 'extremes' pattern as the years go by.

What's the probability next year's record high exceeds 1 unit above the historical baseline?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.genextreme(c=0.1)
print('P(record > 1):', round(dist.sf(1), 4))
print('mean record value:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(record > 1): 0.2944 mean record value: 0.4865
