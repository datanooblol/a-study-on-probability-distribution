Generalized Half-Logistic

scipy.stats.genhalflogistic

Intuition

This stretches the half-logistic distribution's infinite tail into a hard, finite cutoff at 1/c -- useful whenever a quantity behaves like a logistic decay but is physically guaranteed to stop at some maximum value.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \frac{2(1-cx)^{1/c-1}}{\left(1+(1-cx)^{1/c}\right)^2}, \quad 0 \le x \le 1/c \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c0.5shape parameter setting the finite upper bound at 1/c

Use cases

  • Modeling bounded lifetime or decay data with a hard physical limit
  • Truncated hazard-rate modeling in reliability engineering

A funny example

A kid slides down a playground slide whose length is fixed. Their speed decays the way a half-logistic distribution would, but no matter what, they can never travel past the very end of the slide.

What's the probability the kid's measured distance exceeds 1.5 units, given the slide effectively caps out at 1/c = 2?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.genhalflogistic(c=0.5)
print('support upper bound:', round(dist.support()[1], 4))
print('P(X > 1.5):', round(dist.sf(1.5), 4))
support upper bound: 2.0 P(X > 1.5): 0.1176
