Fisk (Log-Logistic)

scipy.stats.fisk

Intuition

Fisk, also called the log-logistic distribution, is what you get if the logarithm of your data follows a logistic curve -- it looks similar to the lognormal but has a heavier, slower-decaying tail, which makes it popular for income and survival data.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \frac{c\,x^{c-1}}{(1+x^c)^2}, \quad x > 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c3.5shape parameter controlling tail heaviness

Use cases

  • Income distribution and economic inequality modeling
  • Survival analysis with a non-monotonic hazard rate

A funny example

A kid tracks how long each of their paper airplanes stays in the air. Most fly for a typical, similar amount of time, but this distribution's heavier tail means the occasional lucky plane rides a breeze for way longer than you'd expect from a plain bell curve.

What's the probability a flight lasts longer than 2 (scaled) time units, and what's the median flight time?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.fisk(c=3.5)
print('P(flight time > 2):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('median flight time:', round(dist.median(), 4))
P(flight time > 2): 0.0812 median flight time: 1.0
