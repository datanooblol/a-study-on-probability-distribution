Exponentiated Weibull

scipy.stats.exponweib

Intuition

This generalizes the Weibull distribution by raising its CDF to a power, which lets it produce a bathtub-shaped hazard rate (high risk early, low in the middle, rising again later) that plain Weibull cannot -- important for modeling real-world equipment lifetimes.

Formula

\[ f(x;a,c) = a\,c\,x^{c-1}\left(1-e^{-x^c}\right)^{a-1}e^{-x^c}, \quad x > 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a2.5exponentiation power applied to the Weibull CDF
c1.5Weibull shape parameter

Use cases

  • Bathtub-curve reliability and failure-time modeling
  • Wind speed and hydrology extreme-value modeling

A funny example

A new video-game console might fail right out of the box due to manufacturing defects, is usually rock-solid for years afterward, then starts wearing out again as it ages -- exactly the up-down-up failure risk this distribution is built to capture.

What's the probability the console survives past 1.5 years, and what's its expected lifetime?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.exponweib(a=2.5, c=1.5)
print('P(survives > 1.5 yrs):', round(dist.sf(1.5), 4))
print('expected lifetime:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(survives > 1.5 yrs): 0.3519 expected lifetime: 1.3466
