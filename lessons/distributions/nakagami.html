Nakagami

scipy.stats.nakagami

Intuition

Developed to model radio signal fading, Nakagami generalizes the Rayleigh distribution (which it equals at nu=1) with a single shape parameter that controls how severely a wireless signal's strength fluctuates as it bounces off buildings and obstacles on its way to your phone.

Formula

\[ f(x;\nu) = \frac{2\nu^\nu}{\Gamma(\nu)}x^{2\nu-1}e^{-\nu x^2}, \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
nu1.5shape parameter (fading/spread parameter, nu >= 0.5)

Use cases

  • Wireless and radio signal fading models in telecommunications engineering
  • Ultrasound and medical imaging signal amplitude modeling

A funny example

A kid's walkie-talkie signal gets stronger and weaker as they walk around a big building, bouncing off walls and corners in unpredictable ways. The Nakagami distribution describes exactly how those signal-strength bounces are spread out.

What's the probability the signal strength exceeds 1.5 units?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.nakagami(nu=1.5)
print('P(signal > 1.5):', round(dist.sf(1.5), 4))
print('mean signal strength:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(signal > 1.5): 0.0803 mean signal strength: 0.9213
