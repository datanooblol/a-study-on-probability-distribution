Pearson Type III

scipy.stats.pearson3

Intuition

Pearson Type III is a shifted, scaled gamma distribution reparameterized directly in terms of skewness, letting you dial in exactly how lopsided the curve is -- it's the officially recommended distribution for U.S. flood-frequency analysis (as the log-Pearson III) because real river flow data is rarely symmetric.

Formula

\[ f(x;\gamma) = \frac{|\beta|}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigl(\beta(x-\zeta)\bigr)^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta(x-\zeta)}, \quad \beta=\frac{2}{\gamma},\ \alpha=\frac{4}{\gamma^2},\ \zeta=-\frac{\alpha}{\beta} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
skew1.5skewness of the distribution (0 recovers the normal distribution)

Use cases

  • Flood-frequency analysis (log-Pearson III is the US federal standard)
  • Hydrology and streamflow data modeling

A funny example

A kid records the water level of a creek every spring. Most years it's a fairly typical level, occasionally it's notably higher, but it almost never drops far below normal -- the skew parameter dials in exactly that kind of lopsided spread.

What's the probability the water level exceeds 2 units above baseline?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.pearson3(skew=1.5)
print('P(level > 2):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('mean level:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(level > 2): 0.0473 mean level: 0.0
