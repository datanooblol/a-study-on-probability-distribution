Maxwell

scipy.stats.maxwell

Intuition

The Maxwell (Maxwell-Boltzmann) distribution is the speed of a gas molecule bouncing around in three dimensions when each of its velocity components is an independent normal random variable -- it's the physics behind why gas molecules have a most-likely speed but a long tail of much faster outliers.

Formula

\[ f(x) = \sqrt{2/\pi}\,x^2 e^{-x^2/2}, \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning

Use cases

  • Kinetic theory of gases: molecular speed distribution in physics
  • Modeling 3D vector magnitudes from independent normal components

A funny example

Imagine a room full of bouncy balls zipping around randomly in all directions, each direction's speed component wobbling like a bell curve. The overall speed of any one ball (never negative, since speed can't be negative) follows the Maxwell distribution.

What's the probability a ball's speed exceeds 2.5 units, and what's its expected speed?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.maxwell()
print('P(speed > 2.5):', round(dist.sf(2.5), 4))
print('expected speed:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(speed > 2.5): 0.1001 expected speed: 1.5958
