Chi

scipy.stats.chi

Intuition

If you take k independent standard-normal 'wiggles' (one per dimension), the chi distribution is the length of the resulting vector -- the square root of a chi-squared random variable. Maxwell and Rayleigh are just chi with 3 and 2 degrees of freedom.

Formula

\[ f(x;k) = \frac{x^{k-1}e^{-x^2/2}}{2^{k/2-1}\Gamma(k/2)}, \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
df5degrees of freedom

Use cases

  • Distance/magnitude of a multivariate normal vector from its mean
  • Deriving standard deviation estimator distributions in statistics

A funny example

A robot arm has 5 independent motors, each nudging it a tiny random bit in its own direction, like little bell-curve wiggles. The chi distribution tells you how far the arm's tip ends up from where it was supposed to be, in total.

What's the probability the tip ends up more than 3 units from its target, and what's the expected total drift?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.chi(df=5)
print('P(distance > 3):', round(dist.sf(3), 4))
print('expected distance:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(distance > 3): 0.1091 expected distance: 2.1277
