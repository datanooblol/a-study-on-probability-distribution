Jones-Faddy Skew-t

scipy.stats.jf_skew_t

Intuition

Jones and Faddy's skew-t distribution generalizes the Student's t distribution by letting the two tails have independently different weights (a controls one side, b the other) -- when a equals b it reduces exactly to a symmetric t distribution with 2a degrees of freedom.

Formula

\[ f(x;a,b) = C_{a,b}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{a+b+x^2}}\right)^{a+1/2}\left(1-\frac{x}{\sqrt{a+b+x^2}}\right)^{b+1/2} \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a2left-tail shape parameter
b8right-tail shape parameter

Use cases

  • Skewed financial return modeling with independently tunable tails
  • Robust regression error modeling with asymmetric outlier risk

A funny example

A kid's grades on quizzes usually cluster tightly around their average, but they're more likely to have one really bad surprise quiz than one incredibly lucky one -- the a and b knobs here let you dial in exactly how lopsided that surprise risk is.

What's the probability a quiz score falls more than 2 units below average?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.jf_skew_t(a=2, b=8)
print('P(X < -2):', round(dist.cdf(-2), 4))
print('mean:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(X < -2): 0.6563 mean: -3.1215
