Landau

scipy.stats.landau

Intuition

The Landau distribution describes the energy lost by a fast charged particle passing through a thin layer of material -- most collisions cost a little energy, but occasionally one violent collision costs a lot, giving a sharp peak with an extremely long, heavy right tail (it doesn't even have a finite mean).

Formula

\[ f(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-t\ln t - xt}\sin(\pi t)\,dt \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning

Use cases

  • Modeling energy loss (ionization) of charged particles in detector physics
  • Heavy-tailed fluctuation modeling where no finite mean exists

A funny example

A pinball rockets through a row of bumpers. Most of the time it loses a modest, predictable bit of speed at each bumper, but every so often it clips one bumper dead-on and loses a huge, unpredictable chunk of speed all at once.

What's the probability the energy loss is below 1 (in standardized units)?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.landau()
print('P(X < 1):', round(dist.cdf(1), 4))
print('mode (most likely loss):', round(dist.pdf(0), 4))
P(X < 1): 0.5779 mode (most likely loss): 0.2622
