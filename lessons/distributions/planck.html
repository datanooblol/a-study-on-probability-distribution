Planck

scipy.stats.planck

Intuition

This is the discrete exponential distribution on all non-negative integers, named for its role in Planck's law of blackbody radiation describing how photon energy states are occupied -- it's mathematically identical to a geometric distribution, just re-parameterized in terms of a decay rate.

Formula

\[ f(k;\lambda) = (1-e^{-\lambda})e^{-\lambda k}, \quad k=0,1,2,\ldots \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
lambda_0.5decay rate parameter (higher = faster drop-off)

Use cases

  • Blackbody radiation photon occupation number modeling in physics
  • Discrete waiting-time and count modeling equivalent to the geometric distribution

A funny example

A vending machine dispenses a random number of extra bonus stickers with each purchase, and getting 0 extra stickers is most common, getting 1 extra is less common, and so on, dropping off at a steady exponential rate the more stickers you'd need.

What's the probability of getting exactly 2 bonus stickers?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.planck(lambda_=0.5)
print('P(exactly 2 stickers):', round(dist.pmf(2), 4))
print('mean stickers:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(exactly 2 stickers): 0.1447 mean stickers: 1.5415
Theoretical shape at the default parameters above (blue = density/PMF, orange = CDF).
