Logarithmic Series

scipy.stats.logser

Intuition

The logarithmic series distribution is the classic model in ecology for 'species abundance' -- most species in a habitat are observed just once or a few times, and progressively fewer species are seen many times, matching exactly this rapidly-decaying pattern.

Formula

\[ f(k;p) = \frac{-p^k}{k\ln(1-p)}, \quad k=1,2,3,\ldots \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
p0.6shape parameter, 0 < p < 1

Use cases

  • Species abundance modeling in ecology (Fisher's logarithmic series)
  • Word-frequency and linguistic corpus modeling

A funny example

A kid on a nature walk tallies how many times they spot each type of bug. Most bug species get spotted just once, a fair number get spotted twice, and only a rare few super-common bugs get spotted many, many times.

What's the probability a given bug species was spotted exactly 3 times?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.logser(p=0.6)
print('P(spotted exactly 3 times):', round(dist.pmf(3), 4))
print('mean count:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(spotted exactly 3 times): 0.0786 mean count: 1.637
