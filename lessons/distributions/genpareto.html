Generalized Pareto

scipy.stats.genpareto

Intuition

This is the universal shape that the tail of almost any distribution converges to once you look only at values above a very high threshold -- the mathematical backbone of 'peaks over threshold' extreme-value risk analysis, from insurance losses to flood levels.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = (1+cx)^{-1-1/c}, \quad x \ge 0,\ c \ne 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c0.3shape parameter (0 gives exponential tail, positive gives heavy Pareto tail, negative gives bounded support)

Use cases

  • Peaks-over-threshold extreme value analysis for insurance and flood risk
  • Modeling exceedances in financial risk (Value at Risk)

A funny example

A theme park logs the wait times that are unusually long -- only counting waits already past 30 minutes. However bumpy the full wait-time data looks overall, these extreme long waits themselves settle into this one universal 'excess over threshold' shape.

Given a wait is already past the threshold, what's the probability the excess wait exceeds 2 more units, and what's the expected excess wait?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.genpareto(c=0.3)
print('P(excess > 2):', round(dist.sf(2), 4))
print('expected excess:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(excess > 2): 0.2087 expected excess: 1.4286
