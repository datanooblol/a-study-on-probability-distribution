Generalized Exponential

scipy.stats.genexpon

Intuition

This generalizes the plain exponential distribution by letting the 'risk rate' change over time instead of staying constant -- risk starts elevated and settles down toward a steady baseline, useful for anything with an early burn-in period of extra risk.

Formula

\[ f(x;a,b,c) = \left(a+b\left(1-e^{-cx}\right)\right)\exp\!\left(-ax-bx+\frac{b}{c}\left(1-e^{-cx}\right)\right), \quad x \ge 0 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
a1shape parameter controlling the baseline hazard
b2shape parameter controlling extra early hazard
c3shape parameter controlling how fast the extra hazard fades

Use cases

  • Reliability modeling of systems with an early-life burn-in hazard
  • Survival analysis with time-varying risk

A funny example

A new puppy is extra clumsy and prone to knocking things over during its first few weeks home, but that risk fades and settles into a steady, lower baseline of occasional mischief as it grows up.

What's the probability the puppy causes no more mischief incidents after 1 time unit, and what's the expected time to its next incident?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.genexpon(a=1, b=2, c=3)
print('P(time to incident > 1):', round(dist.sf(1), 4))
print('expected time:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(time to incident > 1): 0.0938 expected time: 0.4739
