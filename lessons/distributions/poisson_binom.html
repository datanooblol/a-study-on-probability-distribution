Poisson Binomial

scipy.stats.poisson_binom

Intuition

This generalizes the binomial distribution by letting every trial have its own different success probability instead of one shared p -- exactly what happens when you sum up several different biased coins (or, for example, several different students' independent chances of passing a quiz).

Formula

\[ f(k;p_1,\ldots,p_n) = \sum_{A \in F_k}\ \prod_{i \in A}p_i \prod_{j \in A^c}(1-p_j) \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
p[0.2, 0.5, 0.7, 0.3, 0.6]list of individual success probabilities, one per independent trial

Use cases

  • Election and voting outcome modeling with individually-varying voter probabilities
  • Reliability of systems built from non-identical independent components

A funny example

A kid has 5 differently-weighted trick coins in their pocket, each with its own personal chance of landing heads (20%, 50%, 70%, 30%, and 60%). They flip all 5 once and count the total number of heads.

What's the probability exactly 3 of the 5 coins land heads?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.poisson_binom(p=[0.2, 0.5, 0.7, 0.3, 0.6])
print('P(exactly 3 heads):', round(dist.pmf(3), 4))
print('expected heads:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(exactly 3 heads): 0.3022 expected heads: 2.3
