Bradford

scipy.stats.bradford

Intuition

Bradford's distribution captures a 'law of scattering' observed in library science: items ranked by relevance or popularity fall off in a specific logarithmic way, with most items squeezed toward the low end of a bounded range.

Formula

\[ f(x;c) = \frac{c}{\ln(1+c)\,(1+cx)}, \quad 0 \le x \le 1 \]
parameterdefault used heremeaning
c3shape parameter controlling how strongly probability is skewed toward small x

Use cases

  • Bradford's law of scattering in library and information science
  • Modeling skewed rank/popularity distributions

A funny example

A librarian ranks 100 books from most to least borrowed and marks each one's position on a number line from 0 to 1. Way more books cluster near the popular end than spread evenly across the shelf.

What fraction of books are expected to fall in the top (lowest x) 20% of the ranked line?

Solve it with scipy.stats

import scipy.stats as stats
import numpy as np

dist = stats.bradford(c=3)
print('P(X < 0.2):', round(dist.cdf(0.2), 4))
print('mean rank position:', round(dist.mean(), 4))
P(X < 0.2): 0.339 mean rank position: 0.388
